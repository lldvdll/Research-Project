## Top fifteen findings, ranked

Substantive claims about the phenomenon — each stands on its own terms, independent of how it came
to be measured. Score = importance × strength of the evidence *in this document*; every entry is
re-derived from the saved arrays. Nothing scores 10.

1. **9/10 · strong** — **Predictive coding does not reduce forgetting in Domain-IL,** at any depth or width tested and under both fixed-budget and matched-competence readings (52, 53, 59, 74).
2. **9/10 · strong** — **Endpoint retention metrics invert the ranking of two rules depending on where training stops; crossover height does not** — the measured direction of an effect is a function of the stopping point (110, 111, 112).
3. **8/10 · strong** — **Class-IL forgetting is a readout failure, not a loss of representation:** the hidden code survives almost intact while argmax reads the floor (42, 131, 320).
4. **8/10 · strong** — **Protecting the output layer does not recover Class-IL retention** — freezing it changes crossover by nothing, in either scenario (130).
5. **8/10 · strong** — **A memory mechanism solves what credit-assignment rules do not:** replay separates in every cell tested, so the problem is solvable and the energy-based rules fail at something achievable (52, 56, 59, 53, 57).
6. **8/10 · strong** — **EqProp forgets the most and is measurably the least efficient per synapse** — it travels furthest to arrive shortest, so its credit assignment is worse rather than merely different (52, 53, 56, 57, 54, 65).
7. **7/10 · strong** — **PC's only advantage is Class-IL, and its output-layer edge is largest in the scenario where that advantage is absent** — the layer-based explanation for the gain does not hold (56, 57, 112, 66).
8. **7/10 · strong** — **Sparsity gating harms retention, and harms PC several times more than backprop** (220).
9. **7/10 · strong** — **Added consolidation buys only small gains, and past a threshold the penalty deadlocks learning entirely** (200, 210).
10. **7/10 · strong** — **Task difficulty is a property of the class split rather than the initialisation,** and particular digits shift Class-IL retention systematically (302, 312, 313).
11. **7/10 · strong** — **How hard absent classes are pushed down is set by the loss and target coding, but masking removes the suppression regardless of coding** (120).
12. **7/10 · moderate** — **Target alignment does not track forgetting and ranks the rules opposite to retention** — the mechanism credited in the source literature does not carry the effect (64) — five seeds, magnitude explanation not in the arrays.
13. **6/10 · strong** — **Under repeated task alternation the rules converge and none separates on mean test error,** the memory-based control included (68).
14. **6/10 · strong** — **Capacity is not the binding constraint, and the trunk does substantial work:** a frozen random projection falls well short of the trained ceiling at the same width (100, 101).
15. **5/10 · weak** — **Class-pair similarity at the shared output unit predicts Domain-IL retention** — a large effect in one seed block that did not replicate in an independent one (60, 301, 311).

- **Decision — no prior result carried forward:** the pre-protocol experiments varied width, biases and stopping rule between themselves, and the headline run's positive control inverted on re-run (20–27, 61).
